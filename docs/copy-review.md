# Portfolio copy review

Reviewed 25 September 2026. Scope: homepage, navigation, footer, search menu, timeline, process page, three case studies, both articles, four CV variants, metadata, image descriptions and diagram labels. This is an editorial review of the repository content, not independent verification of business results or a technical audit of the tutorial.

## Main finding

The specific projects and production incidents are the strongest evidence of your experience. Repeated claims about AI agents, dense terminology and absolute promises were making that evidence harder to understand. The revised copy introduces you and your work first, then explains the technical process where it is relevant.

## Changes applied

| Area | What changed and why |
| --- | --- |
| Homepage | Introduces Khalil, location, remote experience and the work you do. Replaces “Agents type. I own what ships.” with a calmer headline about building software from the first idea to daily use. |
| Project introductions | Explains what each product does and who uses it before describing implementation. |
| Experience and CVs | Keeps specific results and technical keywords, but removes “AI-native” language, boastful comparisons and repetitive claims of ownership. Each role summary highlights relevant experience. |
| Process page | Describes planning, implementation, review, testing and measurement in plain language. Uses general practices and tool descriptions, without named projects or project-specific metrics. |
| Case studies | Uses clearer headings, explains failure recovery, and replaces guarantees such as “Customers never see it” with descriptions of the mechanism. Keeps business growth attributed to the team. |
| Articles | Simplifies introductions and dense passages, standardises RavenClip branding, and removes repetitive promotional sections from the WSL guide. Technical examples remain unchanged. |
| Navigation and contact | Uses “Experience,” “Download PDF,” and “Find a page or action.” The contact section invites visitors to describe a project, improvement or role. |

## Facts to confirm before publishing

- **RavenClip publishing:** the original engineering article says TikTok and YouTube are live while Instagram awaits Meta review. The case study and CV now follow that account. Confirm whether approval has since arrived.
- **Measurement dates:** add exact snapshot dates to SPS usage totals, its 90-day chart, checkout totals and the AI cart conversion rate. “120 of 120 weekdays” becomes stale without an end date. Define whether a confirmed cart means an accepted suggestion or a paid order.
- **Transport pricing:** €109 to €75 is about a 31.2% reduction; the existing copy says 32%. Confirm whether that percentage uses unrounded source values. The existing figures have been retained pending confirmation.
- **Performance:** explain what “60%” measures at Singularity: response time, throughput or another metric. The timeline currently calls it “60% faster,” which may not describe the same calculation.
- **Overdue balance:** explain whether the reported balance was 13 times too high or too low, and over what period.
- **Country count:** confirm which six countries the homepage total includes; the employment history and client locations use different groupings.
- **Historical tutorial:** the WSL article is explicitly dated as a November 2025 configuration. Its commands and permissions have not been revalidated against current releases in this copy review.

## Editorial direction for future updates

Lead with the user’s problem, describe your contribution, then give the result and its measurement period. Use AI tool names when explaining your process. Keep implementation detail in the case studies and engineering articles, where readers can choose how deep to go. A short, specific account of a decision is more useful than another claim about speed, ownership or code volume.
