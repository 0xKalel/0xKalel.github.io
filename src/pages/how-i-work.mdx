## Decide what matters now

I ask what needs to change, who depends on it, and what a useful first result looks like. Then I separate what is necessary now from what can wait, and break the work into releases that deliver a complete, usable workflow.

For RavenClip, that meant [prioritizing unattended publishing](/work/ravenclip/#start-with-a-complete-workflow) and later adapting the product around its paying customers. At StoragePal, operations screens were among my first priorities because they blocked work staff needed to do every day.

## Make the technical choices fit the context

I consider performance, data correctness, failure recovery, and the cost of maintaining the system. A pattern earns its place by solving a problem we have.

At StoragePal, shared catalogue data can be cached, but [personal cart state stays separate](/work/storagepal/#cache-what-customers-share). For SPS, I [removed offline support](/work/sps/#remove-complexity-the-warehouse-does-not-need) after establishing that every warehouse had internet. Maintaining less code was the useful choice there; scanning without a connection became an accepted limitation.

## Use AI with clear responsibility

AI helps me implement, investigate, and test scoped changes. Product priorities, pricing, architectural tradeoffs, and acceptance criteria remain my responsibility. I write down constraints, challenge proposals, and ask for evidence that the result works.

That responsibility includes learning from failures. An agent-assisted bulk edit once reached RavenClip production with a syntax error. I added a check that PHP files parse and a deployment sweep that stops before migrations if syntax is invalid. Those checks enforce a requirement that instructions alone had failed to protect.

[Read the incident and what changed](/blog/2026/09/25/ravenclip-engineering/#building-with-ai-and-owning-the-mistakes).

## Keep the team informed and follow the result

I make decisions, progress, and problems visible. Specifications and implementation notes give the team enough context to carry work forward, and I stay involved after release.

Errors, staff feedback, costs, and actual use help decide what to improve next. I distinguish a working mechanism from a measured outcome and revisit assumptions when the evidence changes.
