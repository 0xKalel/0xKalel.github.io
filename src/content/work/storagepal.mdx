## A business already in operation

StoragePal customers book collection, storage, and return of their belongings online. Staff use the platform to manage orders, stock, invoices, and payments.

I took over an existing app and improve it in stages. I set the priorities, write the specs, review work, and build major parts myself.

Other developers added reporting, CRM integration, and reservations. I also built the [SPS warehouse app](/work/sps/) end to end.

## Start where staff were blocked

Staff used the inventory and reservation screens every day. Filters could take minutes or time out. Big exports could run the server out of memory.

I moved filtering and paging into database queries and made exports run in batches. Later I brought **twelve backoffice pages** onto one shared system for columns, filters, sorting, and exports.

Some limits remain: a few heavy computed columns cannot be sorted, and some exports still need a filter to finish in time.

## Cache what customers share

The catalog loaded too much data and repeated the same database work on every visit. I first cut queries and added indexes. Then I rebuilt the page on the server from cached catalog data.

The cache stores product data per language. Customer quantities are merged in on each request, and custom items stay outside the shared cache.

Backoffice changes clear the cache, and a 24-hour expiry catches anything missed. Some import paths do not clear it yet.

I also merged the three-page payment flow into one page.

## Sort out what is owed before charging

Unpaid amounts carried over into later invoices, but the system could still treat them as separate debts. Adding more payment retries on top of that could charge the wrong amount.

I set one rule: the current invoice at the head of the chain is the amount owed. The payment engine checks that rule, and any payment already in progress, before it charges.

Then I added retry handling, a review queue for blocked payments, and reminders that check the payment state again before they send.

## Price transport by the real job

I replaced flat regional quotes with an engine that looks at driving distance, vehicle size, crew, and building access. Customer care and logistics helped shape the rules. Collections and returns now use the same engine.

Transport revenue was **34% higher** in the twelve months after launch than in the twelve months before. Order volume and customer mix changed in that time too.

## Keep AI proposals inside the business rules

The shopping assistant suggests items from text or photos. Server code rejects unknown items, limits quantities, and sets the prices. Customers can edit the proposal, and the cart prices every line again.

The model helps the customer describe what they need. The catalog and the pricing rules decide what can be sold and at what price.
