## The business and my role

StoragePal collects customers' belongings, stores them, and brings them back on request. Its platform supports the daily operation: catalogue, pricing, bookings, warehouse inventory, invoicing, payments and internal administration.

I lead development, set the technical roadmap and build a large part of the platform myself. I work with **Monder** (reporting, CRM, lead capture, reservations), **Med** (WordPress site, SEO, tools) and two testers who verify every feature before it ships.

## The transport pricing engine

The old pricing used broad regions and calculated collections and returns differently. I built a shared pricing engine that uses driving distance from Google Maps, the items being moved, vehicle and crew costs, floor level and lift access.

The checkout went from **7 steps to 3**, on one page, with Apple Pay and Google Pay. It has processed **700+ orders** since December 2025.

## An AI cart builder in production

Customers can describe what they want to store or upload photos. A Gemini assistant turns that into a priced cart. Items are checked against the catalogue, and custom items use the same pricing formula as checkout. **42% of conversations end in a confirmed cart.**

## Billing that collects the right amount

I found and fixed a billing bug that could collect the same unpaid balance more than once when it carried over to a later invoice. I then rebuilt payment recovery, covering retries, bank transfers, write-offs, partial payments and reminders.

The new process recovered **77 failed payments** in its first seven weeks. The investigation also revealed that the reported overdue balance was incorrect by a **factor of 13**.

## The warehouse link

The [SPS warehouse app](/work/sps/) runs on live order data through a REST API I designed: orders, returns, order changes with Stripe repricing, and retry handling designed to prevent duplicate charges. **835 orders** and **8,600+ status changes** processed.

## How it was built

I write the requirements and use Claude Code to help implement them. Project instructions document billing rules and endpoint permissions, while hooks check for destructive commands and lint edits. I review each change, and the testers verify it in the app before release.
