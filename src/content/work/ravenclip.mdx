## The story

I wanted to follow AI news without reading twenty tabs a day, so I built a script that turned the day's best story into a short video for myself. Friends asked for their own topics, then for it to post on its own, in their language, on their schedule. That is when a personal tool became RavenClip.

Once it was a product, the real customers turned out to be **businesses**: agencies, consultancies and coaches who need consistent short video on their channels and don't have a video team. First paying customers within weeks of launch.

## How a video happens

RavenClip watches news feeds, picks the one story worth telling, writes a fact-grounded script, voices it, designs the visuals, renders a vertical video with word-by-word captions, and publishes it to TikTok, YouTube Shorts and Instagram Reels. Nobody is in the loop unless the owner wants a review window.

## When a provider dies mid-render

Every external call goes through one shared failover loop. Its rules were each paid for by a production incident, like the fallback that once ran dry for 25 days while every call silently failed over to a lane three times the price. Now the system rests exhausted providers, retries transients, and alerts me with the exact key to replace.

## How it was built

Built with Claude Code and Codex from specs I wrote stage by stage. The agents typed most of the code; I made the architecture calls, reviewed every change, and kept every rule in exactly one place so one pair of hands can hold the whole system: ~130,000 lines of PHP, ~1,450 commits, held together by ~1,300 tests.
