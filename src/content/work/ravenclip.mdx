## Start with a complete workflow

I wanted a simpler way to follow the news myself. The first version made a complete video, with each stage started by hand. By week three, it ran on a schedule and published on its own.

Around then I built a manual story picker and removed it the same day. The priority was a channel that keeps publishing while its owner is busy. More controls could come once that worked.

## Learn from the people who pay

I first pictured creators running news channels. The first three paying customers were an agency, an AI consultancy, and a coach.

So I moved the product toward businesses building an audience around their expertise. I added brand controls and more channels per account. Three customers is an early signal, and I treat it that way.

The first external customer paid **{evidence.ravenclip.daysToCustomer} days** after the first commit. As of 26 September 2026, all thirty posts from those three customers were published automatically.

## Choose architecture around recovery needs

A video passes through several paid services. Restarting everything after one failed step would throw away finished work and repeat costs.

So each stage saves its status, and a runner moves forward whatever is ready. Voice and visuals run in parallel before rendering. Database locks stop two workers from claiming the same stage, and a failed render can reuse the assets it already has. Publishing has its own protection against duplicate posts.

<PipelineRaven />

The price is more state and recovery code to maintain. For a short user-triggered rebuild, I still use a simple job chain: a fixed sequence does not need the full machinery.

## Notice when fallback hides a problem

A provider ran out of credit for 25 days. Fallback kept videos coming, so nothing looked wrong, while calls quietly moved to a more expensive service.

I added provider health checks, clear failure alerts, and a cooldown for empty balances. A daily check runs outside the job queue, so it can still report trouble when the workers themselves are down.

## Define what AI checks can prove

The pipeline checks script structure, length, and that the story stays about its subject. It also protects the approved narration from later changes. Retries are told exactly what to fix. On-screen figures come from data sources, not from the model.

These checks cannot verify every factual claim, so customers keep review controls, and flagged videos wait for approval.

The product supports nineteen content categories and five interface languages. Paying customers so far publish in English and French.

Recorded API spend averaged **$0.36 per rendered video** across 183 videos from 1-25 September. That excludes payment fees and my time.

TikTok and YouTube publishing are live. Instagram is built but waiting on app review.
