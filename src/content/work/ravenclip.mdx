## The story

I wanted to follow AI news without reading twenty tabs a day, so I built a script that turned the day's best story into a short video for myself. Friends asked for their own topics, then for it to post on its own, in their language, on their schedule. That is when a personal tool became RavenClip.

Once it was a product, the real customers turned out to be **businesses**: agencies, consultancies and coaches who need consistent short video on their channels and don't have a video team. The first paying customers joined within weeks of launch.

## How a video happens

RavenClip reads news feeds, selects a story, writes a script based on the source, and adds narration and visuals. It then renders a vertical video with captions and publishes it to TikTok and YouTube Shorts. Owners can choose to review videos before publication. Instagram publishing is integrated but awaiting Meta’s app review.

## Recovering from a provider failure

Every external call goes through one shared failover loop. I developed its rules in response to production incidents. In one case, a fallback provider ran out of credit for 25 days, sending requests to a service that cost three times as much. The system now temporarily skips providers with no credit, retries temporary failures, and sends alerts with the key that needs attention.

## How it was built

I built RavenClip with Claude Code and Codex, writing the requirements stage by stage and reviewing each change. Shared rules for retries, costs and content checks help me maintain the product on my own. The codebase has around 1,300 automated tests.
