## Start with a complete workflow

I wanted a simpler way to follow the news myself. The first version produced a complete video with each stage started manually. By the third week, it could run on a schedule and publish automatically.

At that stage I built a manual story picker and removed it the same day. The immediate priority was a channel that could keep publishing while its owner was busy. More controls could follow once that workflow worked.

## Learn from the people who pay

The audience I initially imagined was creators running news channels. The first three paying customers were an agency, an AI consultancy, and a coach.

That changed the positioning and priorities toward businesses building an audience around their expertise. I added brand controls and support for more channels per account. Three customers are an early signal, and I treat them as such.

The first external customer paid **{evidence.ravenclip.daysToCustomer} days** after the first commit. As of 26 September 2026, all thirty published posts from those three customers had gone through automatic publishing.

## Choose architecture around recovery needs

Unattended generation passes through several paid services. Restarting everything after a failed render would waste completed work and repeat costs.

Each stage has persisted status, and a runner advances whichever stages are ready. Voice and visuals run in parallel before rendering. Stage claims use database locks, and failed renders can reuse existing assets. Publishing has separate protections against duplicate posts.

<PipelineRaven />

The cost is more state and recovery logic to maintain. For a short user-initiated rebuild, I still use a bounded job chain: its fixed sequence does not need the full orchestration mechanism.

## Notice when fallback hides a problem

A provider ran out of credit for 25 days. Fallback kept the pipeline running, but the underlying problem remained unnoticed and calls moved to a more expensive service.

I added provider probes, classified failure alerts, and a cooldown for exhausted balances. A daily check runs outside the job queue so it can still report trouble when workers are down. A completed video alone does not show whether the system is operating as intended.

## Define what AI checks can prove

The pipeline checks script structure, length, subject grounding, and preservation of the approved narration. Retries receive the specific failures they need to correct. On-screen figures come from data sources.

These checks do not verify every factual claim. Customers have review controls, and warned videos wait for approval. The product supports nineteen content categories and five interface languages; paying-customer use is narrower, in English and French.

Recorded direct API spend averaged **$0.36 per rendered video** across 183 videos from 1–25 September. Same-key retries have a known accounting gap, and the figure excludes payment fees and my time. It is a cost observation rather than a profitability claim.

TikTok and YouTube publishing are live. Instagram is implemented but disabled pending app review.
